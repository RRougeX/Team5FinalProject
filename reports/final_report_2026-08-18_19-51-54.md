# Threat-Hunting Report: COOKIES

**Executive Summary**
This report provides an analysis of the COOKIES malware, focusing on its technical behaviors, indicators of compromise, and associated MITRE ATT&CK techniques. The report also offers threat-hunting and detection recommendations to help organizations identify and mitigate potential COOKIES infections.

**Malware Overview**
COOKIES is a type of malware that operates as a backdoor, allowing unauthorized access to infected systems. It has been observed primarily in Cisco Firepower devices running ASA software.

**Technical Behaviors**
- COOKIES functions as a backdoor, enabling remote access to compromised systems.
- It may utilize specific protocols and ports for communication with command-and-control servers.
- The malware can execute arbitrary commands and potentially download additional payloads.

**Indicators of Compromise**
- Presence of unusual network connections to known malicious IP addresses.
- Detection of COOKIES-related files or processes on the system.
- Abnormal system behavior, such as unexpected network traffic or performance degradation.

**MITRE ATT&CK Techniques**
- T1021 - Remote Services: Use of native protocols for command-and-control communication.
- T1059 - Command and Scripting Interpreter: Execution of arbitrary commands on the infected system.
- T1071 - Application Layer Protocol: Use of application layer protocols for communication.

**Threat-Hunting Recommendations**
- Investigate systems for the presence of COOKIES-related files and processes.
- Monitor network traffic for connections to known malicious IP addresses associated with COOKIES.
- Look for signs of unauthorized remote access or command execution on systems.

**Detection Recommendations**
- Implement YARA rules to detect COOKIES malware in disk images or core dumps.
- Use network monitoring tools to identify unusual outbound connections to suspicious IP addresses.
- Regularly scan systems for the presence of COOKIES-related indicators of compromise.
